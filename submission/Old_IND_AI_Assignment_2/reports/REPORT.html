Problem 1: Smart home energy consumption

A. Expert network and inference

The network has edges D -> O, W -> A, O -> A, O -> L, A -> E, and L -> E. Its factorization is

P(W,D,O,A,L,E) = P(W) P(D) P(O|D) P(A|W,O) P(L|O) P(E|A,L).

W is Weather, D is DayType, O is Occupancy, A is AC usage, L is ApplianceLoad, and E is EnergyConsumption. The state labels follow the assignment. The code constructs each CPT explicitly, checks each parent configuration sums to one, and checks the 96-row joint distribution sums to one. Inference sums compatible joint probabilities and divides by the probability of the evidence.

B. Conditional independence

  1. W is independent of D. The simple paths W -> A <- O <- D and W -> A -> E <- L <- O <- D are blocked by colliders A and E respectively.
  2. W is not independent of D given E. Conditioning on E opens the collider E in the second path. E is also a descendant of collider A, so observing E opens the first path.
  3. W is independent of L given O. The path W -> A <- O -> L is blocked by conditioned noncollider O (and its unobserved collider A). The other path W -> A -> E <- L is blocked by the unobserved collider E.

D. Parameter learning

The structure is held fixed for both estimators. For node i with r states, q parent configurations, counts N_ijk, and parent count N_ij:

  • MLE: theta_ijk = N_ijk / N_ij.
  • Bayesian BDeu posterior mean: theta_ijk = (N_ijk + 10/(r*q)) / (N_ij + 10/q).

The equivalent sample size is 10 per node, spread uniformly over all its CPT cells. Parameters are learned for every node, including both roots. State spaces are fixed to the specification. All observed datasets contain every parent configuration, so the uniform fallback for an unidentified MLE row is never used. Full learned CPTs are saved in the six cpts_*.json files.

Which estimator performs better?

For this seeded 500-sample dataset, BDeu has the smaller mean absolute difference: 0.016467440 versus MLE's 0.018827266. Its pseudocounts reduce variance in sparsely observed CPT rows. This comes with shrinkage toward uniform probabilities, which can add bias: BDeu is worse on Q2 and Q4 in this run. It is therefore better on average here, not uniformly better. Both estimators improve in average error across these three dataset sizes. Individual queries need not improve monotonically; Q4 worsens between 5,000 and 20,000 rows. Problem 2: Mars rover sample collection

1. Domain modeling

Domain.pddl declares only :strips and :typing. Types are rover, location, rock, bay, and drill. Predicates record rover, rock and drill locations, directed connectivity, bay ownership and contents, bay emptiness, rock type, and permanent drill possession. move, pick-drill, collect-soft, collect-hard, and deposit implement the four conceptual actions; collect is split into two STRIPS actions to avoid disjunctive or negative preconditions.

Collection requires co-location and an empty owned bay, removes the rock's ground location, and fills that bay. Hard collection additionally requires the drill. Deposit is allowed at the current location, including locations other than Base, as the brief permits. Pickup permanently records possession of the drill; it does not consume a rock storage bay. Negative effects are normal STRIPS deletes; no negative preconditions are used.

2. Problem modeling

Problem.pddl contains Curiosity, two empty bays, Drill1 at SiteE, and the five rocks at their specified sites. Each two-way connection is represented by two facts. Only SiteC -> SiteD is added for the downhill route.

Problem_extended.pddl adds soft Rock6 at SiteE and hard Rock7 at SiteF. Both Base -> SiteB and SiteB -> Base are absent. Goals require all rocks and the rover at Base; both bays must also be empty. Explicit empty-bay goals in the extended problem are consistent with the base mission and already follow from all rocks being on the ground at Base.

3. Search configurations and measured results

Pyperplan 2.1 parses and grounds the submitted PDDL files. Grounded operators are sorted by name, and subprocesses fix PYTHONHASHSEED=0 for repeatable tie-breaking. Every action has unit cost.

  • Satisficing: greedy best-first search with the FF delete-relaxation heuristic.
  • Optimal: A* with the admissible rover lower bound implemented in lower_bound.py. No preferred-operator pruning or weighted A* is used.

Why the A* heuristic is admissible

The handling bound counts two actions per undelivered ground rock (collect, deposit), one per carried rock (deposit), and one drill pickup when an undelivered hard rock requires it. These handling actions are necessary.

For movement, a cached dynamic program computes the shortest directed route from the current location through every remaining ground-rock site and the drill site when needed, ending at Base. It ignores storage capacity and ordering constraints. Any real completion visits these sites, so this is a movement lower bound. A second bound is the ceiling of half the sum of the shortest distances to Base of all undelivered rocks, with carried rocks starting at the rover. Each move transports at most two rocks by one edge.

The heuristic adds the handling bound to the maximum of these movement bounds. Handling and movement use disjoint action types. Hence the total never exceeds the true remaining action count and is zero at the goal. Pyperplan's A* reopens states when a cheaper path is found, so consistency is not needed for the optimality guarantee.

4. Goal verification

Every plan was replayed using the full grounded PDDL preconditions and effects, with static facts and all operators retained. A separate simulator implements the rover's movement, pickup, collection, and deposit rules. The two final states must agree exactly. At every step it checks that the rover has one location, each rock has one location or bay, each bay holds at most one rock, and empty flags match bay contents. Preconditions also check route direction, ownership, co-location, rock type, and drill possession.

All four plans passed both replays. The .trace.txt files show the dynamic state after every action. The original plans finish with Rock1-Rock5 at Base; the extended plans finish with Rock1-Rock7 at Base. Curiosity is at Base and Bay1 and Bay2 are empty in every final state. The drill remains carried. VAL was not used; validation was performed by the two described simulators.

5. Extended scenario

The storm removes only the Base-SiteB route. SiteB remains reachable through SiteD. The optimal plan reaches SiteD by Base -> SiteA -> SiteC -> SiteD, then reaches SiteB from SiteD. On returning, it travels SiteB -> SiteD -> SiteE -> SiteC -> SiteA -> Base. It never uses the closed route or the forbidden direct SiteD -> SiteC move.

6. Plan analysis

The original greedy plan takes 30 actions while A* takes 25. Both perform five collections, five deposits, and one drill pickup; the difference is 19 versus 14 moves. The greedy heuristic chooses locally useful deliveries and creates extra travel. The extended plans take 44 and 40 actions, with 29 and 25 moves respectively; both use seven collections, seven deposits, and one pickup. The extra rocks and route closure increase required travel.

In the original optimal plan, Rock2 and Rock4 share the first return trip. The rover then picks up the drill at step 12, before collecting Rock5 and Rock3 into the two bays. These are returned together. Rock1 is delivered on the final short trip. Every hard-rock collection follows drill pickup.

In the extended optimal plan, Rock4 and Rock2 share the first return trip. The rover picks up the drill at step 9 while carrying them back, so later hard-rock collections are enabled. Rock5 and Rock7 share the next trip from SiteF, followed by Rock6 and Rock3, then Rock1. The directed SiteC -> SiteD slope is used at step 3. Both bays are used whenever a useful pair is available. These are particular optimal plans; another optimal sequence may deliver rocks or choose bays in a different order.

Problem 3: Royal archive access verification

1. Knowledge representation

Constants are mira, scroll_7, and royal_archive. Variables x,y denote people or records as appropriate in this single-sorted FOL encoding.

Predicate Meaning
Archivist(x) x is an archivist
Verifies(x,r) x verifies record r
Trusted(x) x is trusted
Access(x,r) x has access to r
Sealed(r) r is sealed
Permitted(x,r) x may inspect r
Inspects(x,r) x actually inspects r
FindsMark(x,r) x finds a hidden mark in r
Stored(r,a) r is stored in archive a

The formulas are:

  1. forall x,r: (Archivist(x) and Verifies(x,r)) -> Trusted(x).
  2. forall x,r: (Trusted(x) and Access(x,r) and Sealed(r)) -> Permitted(x,r).
  3. forall x,r: (Inspects(x,r) and FindsMark(x,r)) -> Verifies(x,r).
  4. forall r: Stored(r,royal_archive) -> Sealed(r).
  5. Stored(scroll_7,royal_archive).
  6. Access(mira,scroll_7).
  7. Inspects(mira,scroll_7) and FindsMark(mira,scroll_7).
  8. Archivist(mira).

Permitted and Inspects are different predicates. Permission does not imply that an inspection actually occurred.

2. Negate the goal

The goal is Trusted(mira). Add not Trusted(mira) to the knowledge base. The program does not add the positive goal as a fact.

3. Conversion to clause form

The program represents formulas as terms and performs each transformation. Implications become not antecedent or consequent. De Morgan's laws move negations to predicates. For example, the first rule becomes

forall x,r: not Archivist(x) or not Verifies(x,r) or Trusted(x).

Bound variables are renamed freshly for each scope. Clauses use distinct variables across rules, and each resolution parent is copied separately to standardize variables apart again. Variables printed as A,B in different clauses are local to their clauses, not shared across them.

There are no existential quantifiers, so Skolemization introduces no new symbols. Universal quantifiers are omitted in clause form. Disjunction is distributed over conjunction when needed; the conjunction of the two ground facts in formula 7 produces two unit clauses.

4. Prolog implementation

royal_archive_access.pl contains the required eight labeled sections. main/0 prints the formulas, transformation stages, clauses, and proof. resolve/5 selects complementary literals, performs unification with an occurs check, and returns the remaining literals. Set-of-support resolution starts from the negated goal and prefers shorter new resolvents. This is a procedure for the supplied knowledge base, not a claim of a complete general-purpose first-order theorem prover.

The substantive chain is that the observed inspection and mark establish verification, and verification plus archivist status establish trust. Rules about sealing, access and permission are not needed for this conclusion.

6. Proof verification

The saved outputs/proof_output.txt is actual SWI-Prolog output. It ends with C15 = [], the verified conclusion, and a passing self-test message. The program replays every recorded resolution step against its parents. Self-tests check preservation of repeated variables in CNF, ordinary and occurs-check unification, absence of an assumed positive goal, and failure to refute after separately removing the inspection, mark, or archivist fact.
